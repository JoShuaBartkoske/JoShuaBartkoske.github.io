About Me

Growing Up Between Worlds

I grew up a Third Culture Kid (TCK). Born in Boston, I grew up in Almaty, Kazakhstan. After high school, I moved to Bozeman, Montana, where I studied Physics at Montana State University. As a TCK, my life has the blessing of friends across continents. I also love to connect with people from different backgrounds from myself, no matter the background. I believe as humans created in the image of God (Genesis 1:27), we all have a story that is worth understanding.

Learning about the cosmos

My first introduction to astronomy was through books about the Solar System and in the form of the TV miniseries From the Earth to the Moon. Both of these led me to want to learn as much as possible about the world around me and the cosmos up above. I applied myself in school and teachers nurtured that curiosity. From setting water on fire to balancing chemical equations, science was the cornerstore of my curiosity. In college, professors like Dr. Nico Yunes inspired me with their explanations of how the world worked. The discovery of gravitational waves during my freshman year was a high point in my college academics.

Being an international student

After my third year of undergrad, I ended up studying abroad in China at Shanghai Ocean University (上海海洋大学). I spent a year immersing myself in Chinese culture and learning Mandarin Chinese. While I never took my HSK test, I passed all coursework up through HSK4. However, what really made me love the year I spent studying abroad was connecting with people from all over the world. I had friends from Morocco, Uzbekistan, New Caledonia, Cuba, Vietnam, as well as many Chinese students. One of my highlights was getting together with 20 other international students from SHOU and competing at the 12th International Student Dragon Boat Festival Race. We met with more than 200 other international students and raced in friendly competition. Now, as a graduate student in the USA, my home country, I welcome international students and love to engage with them. As Leviticus 19:34 says, "You shall treat the stranger who sojourns with you as the native among you, and you shall love him as yourself..." (ESV).

A return to physics

After spending a year abroad, I came back to the USA for my final year of undergrad at MSU. I finished up my undergrad right as COVID-19 began to ravage the world and we all stopped seeing each other in person. It was in the middle of that year I accepted an offer to the Physics & Astronomy graduate program at the University of Utah and moved down July 1, 2020. Since we had to be at home and 6 feet apart on campus, that year felt surreal as I passed graduate courses and attempted to build a life. A year later, I ended up finding myself in the gamma ray group at Utah with Professors Dave Kieda and Wayne Springer opening the door for me to do research. I started observing with VERITAS and studying the gamma-binary LS I +61 303.

Life keeps on going

While studying at the U (University of Utah), I also spent plenty of time exploring the world around me and building relationships. I ended up visiting all 5 of Utah's National Parks in my first year and also began dating my now-wife. Since that time, I have also explored most of Utah's many ski resorts, hiked and climbed a number of amazing mountains, and found amazing community within the local body of believers. I have also had the amazing blessing of welcoming a daughter into this world and watch her develop and smile and make the funniest faces in the world. I now consider Utah to be home, despite not knowing what life would look like as I moved during a world-wide pandemic.
